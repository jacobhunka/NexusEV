Tesla's Model Y L Reaches America as a Six-Seat, Three-Row Stretch

Out of Spec  ·  Jul 3, 2026

Tesla is bringing the Model Y L to the United States and Puerto Rico, and on an Out of Spec podcast episode the crew of Kyle, Colton, Alex, and Dave dug through Tesla's own spec sheet to sort out what buyers are getting. The L, for long, is a stretched three-row Model Y that first launched in China. It adds about seven inches of length and six inches of wheelbase over the standard car, stands 1.7 inches taller, and comes only as a six-seat, three-row layout with second-row captain's chairs. Tesla lists 325 miles of EPA range, an 83 kWh pack, and a launch price around 63,000 dollars with destination. The group's read is that it is a strong small-family SUV, not a full-size Model X replacement.

The Model Y L lands in a three-row electric segment that has filled out fast, and the hosts spend real time on where it fits. By their measurements it is 195.9 inches long, which puts it between a standard Model Y and larger rivals like the Kia EV9 at about 197 inches and the Rivian R1S near 201. The buyer angle the video keeps circling is efficiency: the L matches or beats those rivals on range while carrying a smaller battery, which is the tradeoff Tesla has long chosen over hauling extra cells. One piece of context the episode does not dwell on is that this car steps into the gap left by the Model X, which the hosts note has ended production, giving Tesla a three-row option again without the X's price or complexity. Colton's point is that a fully loaded L starts near where a lower-trim EV9 does, which reframes the sticker.

On specs, the video reports a curb weight of 4,612 pounds, about 400 pounds more than a premium all-wheel-drive Model Y, a 0 to 60 time of 4.4 seconds, and a 125 mph top speed. The launch series bundles a year of FSD, a year of premium connectivity, a year of free Supercharging, premium paint and interior, free wheel choice, and a tow hitch rated at 3,500 pounds. Alex, pulling from Tesla's service documentation, says the pack uses Panasonic 2170 cells wired 100 in series for roughly 370 volts nominal, a small step up from the standard car, and a smaller bidirectional power conversion unit. The hosts flag comfort details from Tesla's materials, including second-row seats Tesla says can reach 104 degrees within two minutes at a 5 degree ambient, a 19-speaker audio setup, and 65-watt USB-C ports in the first two rows. Tesla's materials, read out on the show, put third-row legroom at 31 inches and headroom at 38, with heat and dedicated vents back there, and claim 16 percent more cargo volume than the standard car along with a 0.216 drag coefficient. The hosts also note an integrated rear spoiler that relocates the brake light to free up third-row headroom. The episode also covers vehicle-to-load output of about 2.4 kW through a NACS outlet adapter, and Tesla says a Powerwall 3 can tap the car's pack to extend home backup. First deliveries are expected in September.

Bottom line: The Model Y L looks like the Model Y that families actually asked for, and the launch pricing is the real story. At roughly 2,000 dollars more than the Model Y Juniper launch series the hosts reference, a stretched three-row with FSD and a tow hitch thrown in is aggressive against the EV9 and Ioniq 9. The honest caveat is that this is still a Model Y underneath, not a big American three-row, so anyone expecting Suburban space will be tight in the way back. For a growing family that wants a Tesla, though, this is the one worth waiting for.
