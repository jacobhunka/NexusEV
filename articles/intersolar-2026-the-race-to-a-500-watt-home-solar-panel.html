Intersolar 2026: The Race to a 500 Watt Home Solar Panel

Artisan Electrics  ·  Jul 2, 2026

Solar's headline race is no longer only about price, it is about how much power a single roof panel can produce. At Intersolar in Munich, the Artisan Electrics team reports that Aiko showed what it calls the first standard size panel to reach 500 watts, helped by an efficiency figure of around 25 percent, plus a 40 year warranty and an all black design. Rival JA Solar was close behind with a 495 watt standard size panel at 24.8 percent. The video frames the show, one of the largest renewable energy events with thousands of exhibitors across many halls, as a snapshot of where home and commercial solar is heading, from foldable portable panels to vertical arrays, solar roof tiles, and a growing wave of plug in solar kits.

The reason a 500 watt standard size panel matters is space. Mainstream residential panels have generally sat below that on both efficiency and per panel output, so squeezing more watts out of the same footprint means a typical roof can generate more without adding area, which is the constraint most homeowners actually hit. The video also surfaces a UK specific catch that buyers rarely hear about. The host, who runs an installation company, says several of the most interesting products, including certain wall mounted systems and solar roof tiles, are not available in Britain because of certification hurdles such as MCS approval. That is a genuinely useful buyer consideration. The panel with the best spec sheet at a European trade show is not automatically the panel you can legally have fitted at home, and the gap between what exists and what is certified can stretch to years.

Beyond the panel race, the video is a tour of adjacent ideas. It shows a device that diverts surplus solar into crypto mining rather than exporting it to the grid, high voltage cables switching from copper to aluminium as copper prices climb, and a robot that the manufacturer says can lay around 600 panels a day on large ground mount sites. On storage and charging, the host highlights a stackable home battery from Solar Edge in 5 kWh modules, an AI energy assistant the company claims could save money through smarter optimisation, and several plug in solar kits aimed at balconies and rentals. He also reports, from conversations with Tesla staff, that a three phase Power Wall is expected in the UK around early 2027 and that Power Wall 2 and 3 systems will become compatible, which he presents as unconfirmed on timing. It is a lot of announcements, and the video is upfront that many are still prototypes. Elsewhere the tour takes in agrivoltaics, where semi transparent panels shade a greenhouse while generating power, an automated tool that scans panels for hail damage, and a megawatt class charger the host measures at around 1.4 MW for heavy vehicles. There are also compact plug in battery and inverter kits from several brands, aimed at balconies and rentals where a full rooftop install is not an option, a category the host says is drawing a lot of attention this year.

Bottom line: Trade shows reward the shiniest spec, so read the 500 watt milestone as a direction rather than a shopping list. The more practical takeaway is the one the host keeps returning to: the best hardware often lands in the UK late, or not at all, because of certification. If you are planning a system this year, chase what your installer can actually source and certify now, not the panel that stole the show in Munich. The efficiency race is real and it is moving fast, but for a homeowner, availability is what actually shows up on the bill.
