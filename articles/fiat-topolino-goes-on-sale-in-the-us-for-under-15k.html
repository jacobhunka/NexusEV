The Fiat Topolino Goes on Sale in the US for Under $15,000

YOUCAR  ·  Jul 9, 2026

Fiat has started selling the Topolino in the United States, and the specification reads like a typo. Car and Driver reports a starting price of $14,985, a 5-kWh battery, 8 horsepower, and 46 miles of range. The same report puts it at 99.6 inches long and 1,073 pounds, which is shorter and lighter than anything else wearing a car badge here. It also cannot legally be driven on an American public road yet. Per Car and Driver, Fiat says a conversion kit arriving in fall 2026 will requalify the Topolino as a Low Speed Vehicle and lift its top speed from 19 mph to 25 mph, at no extra cost. Until that lands, it is a private-property machine. YOUCAR's walkaround, filmed at the European launch, is still the clearest look at the car itself. The first US cars came off a ship roughly a week ago, according to the outlet, and orders are open now.

The Topolino is not competing with cars. It is competing with golf carts and neighborhood electric vehicles, and that is the only comparison in which the price makes sense. No new street-legal EV sold in the US today lands anywhere near $15,000, while a well-equipped LSV cart can. Car and Driver notes the Topolino has been on sale in Europe since 2023, where it is classified as a quadricycle, a category American vehicle law does not have. Hence the workaround with the kit. What a buyer should weigh is the charging picture. Car and Driver reports the pack refills in around five hours on a 2.3-kilowatt AC connection, and there is no DC fast charging anywhere in the story. That is fine for a beach town runabout plugged in overnight in a garage. It is not fine as anyone's only vehicle, and Fiat is not pretending otherwise: the company suggests resorts, gated communities, country clubs, and downtown areas. It is worth knowing what the Low Speed Vehicle category actually buys you before the kit arrives. US rules for that class generally confine these vehicles to roads with low posted speed limits, and state law varies. A 25 mph machine is legal on a beach town side street and useless on the road that leads to it.

There are two versions and one price. Car and Driver reports the standard Topolino has asymmetrical doors hinged on opposite sides and a panoramic glass roof, while the Dolce Vita drops the doors for rope barriers and a roll-up soft top. Both cost $14,985, and the outlet says the only real decision is whether you want doors and a hard roof or neither. Everything else is fixed, including the green paint and the retro 14-inch wheel covers. Inside, the outlet lists a digital gauge cluster, a phone holder and a USB-C port, with a luggage rack at the rear. LED headlights and taillights, seat belts and side mirrors are standard. The street-legal kit adds a rearview mirror, a backup camera and a pedestrian alert system. Orders are open now with a $2,500 deposit, according to Car and Driver, on limited 2026 quantities through select dealers. YOUCAR's video dates from the Topolino's European debut, when Fiat pitched it as an urban mobility product for younger buyers and city dwellers rather than a novelty. Nothing about the car changed on the way here, only the legal category it had to fit. The US has no equivalent to the quadricycle class, which is the whole reason a free conversion kit exists.

Bottom line: this is a toy, it is priced like one, and that is not an insult. Fifteen thousand dollars buys a lot of golf cart, and none of them look like this or wear a Fiat badge. The catch is the asterisk. You are buying a vehicle that cannot legally use a public road until a kit that does not exist yet arrives, and you are trusting Fiat to ship it for free, on time, to everyone. If you have a gated community and a garage outlet, order one and enjoy it. If you were hoping this was the cheap commuter America keeps asking for, it is not.
