XPENG's L03 Takes China's EV Price War to 63 Markets

Everything Electric APAC  ·  Jul 2, 2026

XPENG is aiming its new L03 at buyers well outside China, and the numbers behind it are what make it worth watching. The Everything Electric APAC walkaround says the coupe styled SUV is set to launch in 63 markets within a year, and the host expects it to land under 25,000 dollars, with a China price under 200,000 yuan. It borrows the look of XPENG's P7 in a more mainstream shape, and the video highlights a drag coefficient of 0.228 and a quoted efficiency of 11.5 kWh per 100 km. There is a roughly 60 kWh battery, a pure EV version the video puts at 440 km of WLTP range, and a range extender version quoted at around 1,000 km combined. For a car at this price, that is an unusually complete spec sheet.

The headline is not the styling, it is the price relative to where the car is going. XPENG's MONA sub brand already showed what a cheap, well equipped Chinese EV can do at home, and the L03 is the attempt to repeat that abroad. That ambition runs into a wall in one of its target regions: the European Union has added tariffs on China built EVs, which raises the landing price and complicates the simple undercut that works so well in China. For buyers, the more useful comparison is not another Chinese model but the mainstream electric SUVs from legacy brands, which the host says should worry executives at Ford and Stellantis. If the L03 arrives in Europe at anything close to its home pricing after duties, it pressures a segment that has struggled to build an affordable, efficient electric SUV. That, rather than the design, is the story worth following, because it is about the whole market and not one model.

On the car itself, the video leans positive while flagging where corners were cut. It praises the 15.6 inch central screen, a windscreen projected head up display the host compares to a 50 inch view, and an interior that feels more premium than the price suggests, including a passenger seat with ventilation, massage, and a one press reclined mode. The host notes the L03 uses a 3C charger rated up to 220 kW, which the video frames as enough for most buyers rather than a flash charging headline. Cost saving choices show up too: a manually operated front boot, simple pull up door handles instead of powered ones, and mostly Chinese apps on the launch software, with the overseas version promised in English and with Google's voice assistant. The car also carries a large pool of onboard computing for its driver assistance, which the host says feels fast and responsive. XPENG told the channel the exterior was penned by a former Ferrari designer, a claim the host repeats with some scepticism. On practicality, the video reports a front boot of roughly 120 to 150 litres, rear cargo around 600 litres, and folding rear seats that open up over 1,000 litres. It also points out the blue rear lights seen in China, which signal self driving mode and are not road legal in markets like the UK.

Bottom line: The L03 looks like the clearest test yet of whether China's price advantage survives the trip overseas. At home, a well built electric SUV under 25,000 dollars is close to unbeatable. In Europe, tariffs and dealer networks will decide whether that stays true. If XPENG can keep it cheap after all the friction, the legacy brands really should be nervous, because this is close to the exact car most buyers say they want and few can currently afford. If the price balloons on the way over, it quietly becomes just another decent option in a crowded field.
