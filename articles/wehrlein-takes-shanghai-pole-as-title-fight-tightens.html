Wehrlein Grabs Shanghai Pole as the Title Fight Tightens

Formula E  ·  Jul 4, 2026

Pascal Wehrlein put his Porsche on pole for Round 12 of the 2026 Formula E season in Shanghai, beating championship leader Mitch Evans in the final duel of qualifying. The Formula E broadcast captured a session that thinned out several big names early: both Nissans and both Mahindras went out in the group stage, taking Oliver Rowland and Edoardo Mortara with them. From there the knockout duels ran fast and close, with margins measured in tenths and, in the final, thousandths. Evans topped Group A and Wehrlein topped Group B before the two met for pole, and the German edged it with a clean last sector. The commentary framed it as a timely result for a driver trying to claw back a title deficit with the run-in approaching.

Formula E qualifying runs a group stage followed by one-on-one duels, so a fast single lap matters more than raw pace across a session, and the broadcast leaned on that format to explain the upsets. The context worth adding is the championship math the commentary only gestured at. Wehrlein's pole and its three points cut his gap to Evans to 24 points, per the session's own reporting, with the Shanghai double-header opening the final stretch of the season. Evans, driving for Jaguar, has led the standings through this run, while Rowland, the reigning champion by the broadcast's account, qualified out of position and left points on the table. For a title fight this tight, a rough Saturday qualifying is not fatal, but on a circuit the commentary repeatedly called unforgiving, starting position carries more weight than usual. The group stage did most of the damage to the other contenders: the broadcast noted Rowland complaining of understeer in the Nissan and staying stuck in the pack, while Mortara missed the cut by around two tenths, leaving both of the drivers chasing Evans out before the duels even began.

Wehrlein had signaled his pace before the duels, topping Group B outright while Evans set the Group A benchmark, and the broadcast noted da Costa made the cut on a single flying lap after a scrappy group run. The duels produced the drama. In the quarterfinals, Jake Dennis edged Sebastien Buemi, Evans dispatched Jean-Eric Vergne, Maximilian Gunther beat Antonio Felix da Costa after da Costa ran wide, and Wehrlein saw off Dan Ticktum by two tenths. The semifinals set up Dennis against Evans and Gunther against Wehrlein. Evans found time in the final sector to knock out Dennis by just under two tenths, while Wehrlein pulled away from Gunther by nearly four tenths. That left Evans and Wehrlein, Jaguar against Porsche, for pole. The broadcast described Evans as looking scrappy through the middle of the lap while Wehrlein stayed composed, and the Porsche came out of the final corner with the advantage, taking pole by around two tenths on a lap in the 1:09s. The commentary noted it as Porsche's 13th team pole and Wehrlein's 12th in Formula E, at a point in the season where every starting slot counts. Across the knockout rounds the gaps rarely stretched past a couple of tenths, which is part of why the broadcast kept returning to how little separates the front of this grid on a single lap.

Bottom line: Pole is not a win, and Formula E races rarely play out the way qualifying suggests, thanks to energy management and attack modes. But this was the result Wehrlein needed. Evans has controlled the championship narrative for weeks, and letting his closest challenger start ahead in a compressed double-header hands Porsche momentum at the worst possible time. Watch the race for how the top four manage energy, not just the first lap. If Wehrlein converts, the title fight gets genuinely uncomfortable for Jaguar.
