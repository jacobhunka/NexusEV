A $132,000 Macan GTS and the Cost of Every Box Ticked

Out of Spec BITS  ·  Jul 10, 2026

Out of Spec BITS stopped by a Porsche dealer near Raleigh, North Carolina to look at a 2026 Macan GTS that a friend who works there had specced himself, and the video turns into something more useful than a walkaround. It becomes a reading of the window sticker. The host and the salesperson go down the option list guessing prices, and the guesses keep coming in low. The car as configured lands at $132,830 according to the sticker they read on camera, for a vehicle the video puts at 563 horsepower, or 420 kW, drawing on a 100 kWh pack. What that number buys, and what it does not, is the most honest look at premium EV pricing you will see this week.

Porsche's option strategy runs against the industry. Most EV makers bundle everything into two or three trims and let software do the differentiating, partly because a simpler build sheet is cheaper to manufacture. Porsche still sells choice by the item, and the video's hosts treat that as a virtue even while wincing at the prices. Two things a buyer should hold onto. The 100 kWh figure quoted on a window sticker is gross capacity, and the hosts note that usable capacity is the number that actually determines range, a distinction most spec sheets bury. And options rarely come back at resale in proportion to what they cost new. A $3,710 stitching package is a purchase, not an investment. The salesperson in the video also speculates that option prices have risen partly to absorb tariff costs, since a line item reading tariff is a harder sell. That is his read, not a Porsche statement.

The prices they read out are the story. The video reports 22 inch Spyder RS wheels at $3,010, a Burmester audio upgrade at $4,960 on top of the premium package, exclusive manufacturer stitching at $3,710, Sport Chrono at $1,180, headrest crests at $390, door puddle projectors at $420, Dolomite Silver paint at $730, and a trailer hitch at $750. On towing, they say Porsche re rated the Macan from 4,400 to roughly 6,000 pounds for 2026 with no mechanical change, though they hedge on the exact figure. The 2026 Macan still uses a CCS port rather than NACS, which the host argues is fine because adapters throttle charging, and the video reports a 40 amp onboard AC charger. A charging demonstration on camera shows the car pulling around 260 kW with no visible ramp up. Porsche's own NACS DC adapter, they say, sells for $180 at a dealer. Two options get real praise. The passenger display dims to nothing from the driver's seat once the car is in drive, which the host calls one of the better implementations of the idea. The augmented reality head up display, rare on a Macan, gets described as enormous and genuinely useful. The salesperson also says the Taycan Cross Turismo, the wagon body style, is being discontinued, and that his store has one unsold example left.

Bottom line: The Macan GTS is the trim to have and it is also the clearest illustration of what you are actually buying at this price, which is not performance. Performance is largely done by the base car. You are buying discretion over eighty small decisions, and Porsche has priced that privilege with a straight face. If that sounds absurd, it is, and it is also the only mainstream EV where anyone offers it. Buy the GTS, skip the crest, and spend the $3,710 stitching money on tires and a set of winters. You will feel those every single day. Nobody has ever felt a headrest crest.
