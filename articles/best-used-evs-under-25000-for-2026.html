Four Used EVs Worth Buying for Under $25,000 in 2026

Ever  ·  Jul 4, 2026

Ever's used-EV guide sets a firm budget, $25,000, and names four cars its host says he has either owned or lived with for at least a couple of months each: the Ford Mustang Mach-E, the Hyundai Ioniq 5, the Tesla Model 3, and the Polestar 2. The pitch is that $25,000 now buys a genuinely good electric car on the secondhand market, not a compromise. Andrew, the host and a project engineer at Ever, walks through range, charging, interior, and the known reliability issues for each, then adds a handful of runner-ups. It plays less as a ranking than a shortlist, with the argument that the right pick depends on whether you value range, charging speed, software, or driving feel most.

The useful framing the video builds, and the reason a guide like this matters now, is that lease returns and steep early depreciation have pushed 2021 to 2024 EVs into territory where they undercut almost any comparable new gas car. The host reports you can find 2024 Ioniq 5 base trims and 2022 to 2023 Model 3s under the cap with low miles. One buyer factor the video handles well is battery chemistry: he singles out the 2022 to 2023 Model 3 rear-wheel drive specifically for its LFP pack, which he says degrades less and can be charged to 100 percent routinely. The consideration the video underweights is home charging. Every one of these cars is a far better deal if you can plug in overnight at home, and public-charging reliability still varies widely by region, which can quietly change which of these four actually fits your life.

The specifics are where the video earns its keep. The host reports the Mach-E in California Route 1 trim pairs the extended-range battery with a single rear motor and 290 horsepower, though he flags a high-voltage junction box recall on 2021 to 2022 extended-range cars that Ford has largely addressed. The Ioniq 5's headline is its 800-volt architecture, which he clocks at 10 to 80 percent in 18 minutes and up to 303 miles of range, offset by a 2024 interior he calls a downgrade from the prior year. The Model 3 he pegs at 272 miles and a roughly 31-minute charge, with a common front control-arm noise he says cost him about $250 to fix. The Polestar 2 gets credit for design and its Google-based route planner, with the caveats of tight rear space and sluggish infotainment. He adds that the Mach-E offers 29.7 cubic feet of cargo plus a 4.7 cubic foot frunk, that an extended-range all-wheel drive Mach-E can hit 60 in about 4.8 seconds if you can find one under the cap, and that Hyundai is warranting the Ioniq 5's known ICCU power-control issue for 15 years or 180,000 miles. On the Model 3 he calls out matrix LED headlights and built-in trip planning as real perks. He warns that the Polestar 2's nicer features are locked behind option packs, so a well-equipped used one with the Plus, Pilot, or Napa packages is worth hunting for over a bare base car. Runner-ups include the Kia EV6, Ioniq 6, Nissan Ariya, and Audi e-tron, with a part two promised.

Bottom line: If you have home charging and want the fewest surprises, the LFP Model 3 is the easy call, and the host clearly leans that way on the battery alone. If charging speed on road trips is your priority, the Ioniq 5's 18-minute window is still hard to beat at this price. The Mach-E is the value play if you find a fixed extended-range car, and the Polestar is for people buying with their eyes. The real headline is that none of these is a sacrifice anymore. Twenty-five thousand dollars buys a real EV now.
