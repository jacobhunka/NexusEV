The Model Y L Reaches America as Tesla Posts a Record Quarter

Kim Java  ·  Jul 6, 2026

Tesla's week had a lot moving at once, and the podcast from Kim Java walks through most of it. Orders are now open in the United States for the Model Y L, the stretched three-row version tested earlier in China, with first deliveries said to be early September 2026 and pilot builds already underway at Giga Texas. The hosts put the launch edition around 62,690 dollars with a free paint choice, a year of Supercharging, and a year of premium connectivity included, and expect regular pricing to settle in the mid 50,000s. On top of the new model, Tesla reported what the hosts describe as its best-ever second quarter, a fresh Cybercab first-responder guide leaked new details, and a facial-verification requirement for Full Self-Driving surfaced in decompiled app code.

The Model Y L matters because of what it replaces. Tesla ended Model X production, and this car slots into the same three-row family space for far less money. The hosts describe a six-seat, captain's-chair layout in a body about seven inches longer than the standard Model Y, with 89 cubic feet of cargo, a second-gen adaptive suspension rather than air, upgraded acoustic glass, and vehicle-to-load power sharing that, until now, only the Cybertruck offered among Tesla's North American lineup. Worth adding from outside the video: three-row electric SUVs remain thin on the ground, and rivals like the Kia EV9 and Rivian R1S sit well above this expected price, which is the gap Tesla is aiming at. The hosts list 325 miles of range on the 19-inch wheel, an 83 kilowatt hour pack, and 250 kilowatt charging.

The software news is where the video gets contentious. Kim Java reports that decompiled code points to Tesla using the cabin camera for facial verification before FSD can be engaged, which the hosts frame as an anti-misuse and fleet-management measure rather than a driving upgrade. They also cover "full self-hearing," described in an interview with engineer Lars Moravi as an in-factory system that uses the car's microphones and AI to catch rattles and squeaks before delivery. Elsewhere, the hosts point to serious reporting that SpaceX is in discussions around T-Mobile and a Starlink-branded mobile service, and note that FSD V14 Lite is rolling out to older Hardware 3 cars after Tesla reportedly compressed the neural net to a fraction of its original size.

The numbers behind the record quarter are the part worth sitting with. Kim Java reports Tesla delivered nearly half a million cars globally in the second quarter, roughly 470,000 of them Model 3 and Model Y, for what the hosts call the company's best-ever Q2 and a figure they say beat Wall Street by tens of thousands of cars. They put the result up about 25 percent year over year, with energy storage deployments around 13.5 gigawatt hours, up roughly 40 percent. What makes it stand out, per the video, is the contrast: the hosts cite Lucid down about 40 percent and Ford's EV sales down about 41 percent over the same window, with Rivian a rare gainer. The Cybercab first-responder guide, meanwhile, revealed a 48-volt accessory system, a 400-volt pack rather than 800, USB-C ports in place of wireless charging, and an external microphone on the B-pillar for remote support.

Bottom line: The Model Y L is the story that will actually move metal. A three-row Tesla in the mid 50,000s, with power sharing and the newest hardware, is the family EV a lot of buyers have been waiting for, and it lands the same week Tesla says it just had its strongest quarter in years. The facial-verification and always-listening items are the ones to keep an eye on. Both are defensible on paper, and both will test how much cabin monitoring owners are willing to accept before it starts to feel like a cost rather than a feature.
