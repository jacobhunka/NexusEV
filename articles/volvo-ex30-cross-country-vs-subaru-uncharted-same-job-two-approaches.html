Volvo EX30 Cross Country vs Subaru Uncharted: Same Job, Two Approaches

Out of Spec BITS  ·  Jul 13, 2026

Out of Spec BITS parks the 2026 Volvo EX30 Cross Country next to the 2026 Subaru Uncharted AWD, two small electric crossovers pitched at buyers who want a little rough-road ability without going full off-roader. The reviewers, who had each been living with one for a week, land on a clear read: these cars aim at the same buyer and get there very differently. The Volvo is the quicker, plusher, more urban-luxury pick, while the Subaru is the roomier, more practical family option. Both wear the plastic cladding and raised, adventure-flavoured styling the segment now expects. Both sit, in Canada, in the low $50,000 range, which is what makes the comparison a genuine head-to-head rather than a mismatch of price and purpose.

A detail the video does not get into is where the Uncharted comes from. It is jointly developed with Toyota and is effectively the twin of the 2026 Toyota C-HR, sharing a platform, a 74.7 kWh battery, and its 338 hp dual-motor setup. In the United States, where the video's Canadian pricing does not directly apply, Subaru lists the Uncharted from around $34,995, with the top GT closer to $44,000, and quotes over 300 miles of range on the base front-drive version. That US framing matters because it sets the Subaru against rivals like the Chevrolet Equinox EV and the redesigned Nissan Leaf, not just the Volvo. The EX30 Cross Country, by contrast, has no direct twin and competes on design and brand feel more than on raw value, which is roughly the split the reviewers describe.

On the numbers, the reviewers cite a 69 kWh pack and 422 hp for the EX30 Cross Country, with Volvo quoting 0 to 100 km/h in 3.7 seconds, against the Uncharted AWD's 338 hp and a 77 kWh pack. They put DC charging close, roughly 26 to 27 minutes to 80% for the Volvo and about half an hour for the Subaru, with both using a native NACS port and both eligible for a $5,000 Canadian rebate. The Subaru wins on rear-seat room and adds an onboard 120-volt outlet, while the reviewers note Volvo plans to add vehicle-to-load through a software update. They praise the EX30's flax-cloth interior, standard Harman Kardon audio, and softer ride, but flag a tight rear seat. Impressions and figures are as presented by the channel.

Bottom line: Pick by priority, not by badge. If you carry people and want space, an onboard outlet, and Subaru's all-weather reputation, the Uncharted is the sensible family buy. If it is mostly you, city miles, and how a car feels matters, the EX30 Cross Country is the nicer thing to live with and seriously quick for the money. The reviewers lean Volvo on charm and Subaru on value, which sounds like a cop-out until you realise it is just honest. There is no wrong answer here, only a question about what you actually do with the car.

Commentary on a third-party video. Figures and claims are as presented in the source and have not been independently verified. Spotted an error? Tell us and we will correct it.
