Scout's Traveler Spirit of 26: A Closer Look

EddieX  ·  Jul 5, 2026

Scout Motors is still coming, and a walkaround from EddieX gives one of the closest looks yet at the Traveler SUV, shown here as a patriotic concept called the Spirit of 26. Filmed at Scout's innovation center in Novi, Michigan, the video covers the exterior, interior and the powertrain question that keeps tripping people up. The headline: these are not electric-only. Alongside a pure battery version, Scout offers the Harvester, an extended-range setup the video describes as a 1.4-liter gasoline four-cylinder that acts purely as a generator and never drives the wheels. Scout is targeting up to 350 miles of range for the electric Traveler and at least 500 with the Harvester, on a body-on-frame platform with 35-inch tires, front and rear lockers and a disconnecting sway bar.

The interesting part is how hard buyers are leaning toward the range extender. The video reports around 180,000 reservations, with roughly 85 percent choosing the Harvester over the pure EV. That tracks with a broader industry shift: extended-range and plug-in setups are being pitched across the market as a hedge for buyers who want electric driving but not charging anxiety, and Scout is betting heavily that its off-road, towing-focused audience falls in that camp. Because the engine only generates electricity, the video notes, you keep the instant torque and smooth delivery of an EV while carrying a gas tank for the long trips and remote job sites where charging is thin.

Pricing matters here too. The video notes Scout is still aiming to start under 60,000 dollars and points out the federal EV tax credit is gone, which changes the math for any EV in this range. Against a Rivian R1S, which the video calls noticeably more expensive, a sub-60,000-dollar body-on-frame SUV with a gas backup is a genuinely different pitch to mainstream truck buyers. Scout is also leaning on heritage, reviving the Traveler and Terra names and nodding to its International Harvester roots, a lineage most new EV brands cannot claim.

The walkaround spends most of its time on details. The reviewer, who says he is 6 foot 3, describes the Traveler as roughly Tahoe-sized and flags an unusually clean integrated winch, a full-size tire carrier, a split tailgate and a front trunk that stays usable on the Harvester because the engine sits back by the rear axle. Charging is a native Tesla-style NACS port on an 800-volt architecture, with up to 350 kW claimed, plus 120-volt outlets rated at 2,200 watts. The video also lists estimated towing of 10,000 pounds for the Terra truck and 7,000 for the Traveler, and a 0 to 60 time as quick as 3.5 seconds.

Inside, the video praises a deliberately analog cabin: rows of physical toggles, an actual compass and plaid-textured trim, with one large touchscreen rather than screens everywhere. A Scout designer in the video ties the truck's red, white and blue graphics to a 250th-birthday tribute and the brand's heritage. On timing, the video says production is targeted for 2027 with customer deliveries in 2028, the South Carolina plant still under construction, and the model split running about 75 percent Traveler to 25 percent Terra.

Bottom line: Scout keeps saying the right things, and the Harvester is the smartest decision in the whole program, because it answers the one objection that kills rugged EV sales before a test drive. The analog interior is a real differentiator in a market drowning in touchscreens. The catch is time: production in 2027 and deliveries in 2028 is a long wait, and a lot can change on price and spec between a concept in Novi and a truck in a driveway. If they hit under 60,000 dollars with these numbers, it lands. That is still an if.
