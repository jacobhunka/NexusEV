Wehrlein Wins in Shanghai as the Title Fight Narrows

Formula E  ·  Jul 5, 2026

Pascal Wehrlein controlled a rain-threatened Round 12 in Shanghai to take his second win of the Formula E season, and the result reshaped the title race with the weekend's second Shanghai race still to come. Starting near the front, the Porsche driver worked through a chaotic opening, survived a pit-boost shuffle that scrambled the order, and came home ahead of Antonio Felix da Costa and Jake Dennis. The broadcast framed the day around one number: championship leader Mitch Evans arrived with a comfortable cushion and left clinging to a lead of about three points. Dark clouds hung over the circuit all race, and a light shower midway through turned the track slick enough to reorder the field on its own.

What makes this result matter is not the win itself but how thin the margin at the top has become. Formula E decides its champion on cumulative points across the season, so a swing this large with races still to run keeps several drivers mathematically in the fight rather than settling anything. The broadcast reported that Evans came into the weekend with a 19-point lead and left with roughly three, with Wehrlein moving up to second, da Costa third and Dennis fourth in the standings. Eduardo Mortara, by the broadcast's account, was among the day's bigger losers and slipped to sixth overall.

For anyone following the series casually, the takeaway is that the 2026 title is now a genuine multi-car contest heading into the closing rounds, not a procession. It is the kind of tightening that tends to make the remaining races worth watching, even for people who tuned out when one driver looked to be running away with it. Wet weather and mid-race charging only add to the unpredictability, which is exactly what a series wants when a season reaches its business end.

The race itself turned on weather and strategy. According to the broadcast, drivers began triggering pit boost around the end of lap 13, the expected window, which sent the pit lane into a congested scramble and rearranged the running order. Wehrlein and da Costa split their timing from the leaders, and the broadcast credited Wehrlein with grabbing the undercut and emerging in front once the stops cycled through. Attack mode added another layer, with several drivers using theirs early and Mortara left exposed after burning his. There was contact too, notably between Evans and Oliver Rowland as positions changed hands, and Taylor Barnard's race unraveled with a puncture well before the pit window.

The recovery drives were the subplot. The broadcast described Joel Erikson climbing into the points with a late move for seventh, and Mortara fighting up from near the back of the grid to tenth, salvaging a couple of points on a rough day. Evans, the man under the most pressure, came home eighth after what the commentary put down to a car he struggled to balance. In his post-race comments, Wehrlein described a clean, error-free run and said he deliberately stayed out of the slipstream battles behind him. The broadcast called it his second win of the year and the tenth of his Formula E career.

Bottom line: Wehrlein did exactly what a title challenger has to do, which is win cleanly on a day when half the field was gambling on the weather. If you had written off this championship as decided, this is the race that reopens it. Evans is still ahead, but a three-point lead is the kind of margin that evaporates with one bad pit stop or one wet corner. The next round is now must-watch, and Porsche looks like the team carrying the momentum. Watch whether Evans can steady the ship or whether the pressure keeps building.
