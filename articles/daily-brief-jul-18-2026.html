This Week, Everyone Is Arguing About Price

nexusEVnews Daily Brief  ·  Jul 18, 2026

The price of an electric car is being renegotiated in every direction at once, and this week put the whole spread on display in a single day. At one end sits a six figure Porsche that asks you to pay for feel. At the other, a Subaru that undercuts almost everything and a wave of Chinese brands doing the same thing to an entire country. In between are two American luxury names cutting prices for reasons that could not be more different.

Start with the extremes. The base Porsche Cayenne Electric that Out of Spec flew in from Germany lands near $122,000 and openly trades on intangibles, seating position, build quality, and a charging curve, because the spec sheet does not win it the fight. Set that against the Subaru Uncharted GT that State of Charge drove to 255 highway miles, a car whose top trim starts in the low $40,000s and gets cheaper below that. Same category of buyer, wildly different answers to what a dollar of EV should buy.

The middle is where it gets uncomfortable for legacy brands. CNBC's look at the UK shows what happens when nobody builds a tariff wall: Chinese EVs went from under 1% to more than 30% of that market in seven years, mostly on price. The discounting at home rhymes. Polestar is clearing stock because Washington is forcing it out, while Lucid is stacking 0% financing and cash because demand thinned. One is regulation, one is desire, and both end at a lower sticker.

The next six months will test whether feel is still worth a premium. VW's own answer, the ID Buzz camper The Endless Adventure lived in for three days, sells nostalgia and lifestyle at a price its 234 mile range cannot justify on paper. That is the tell. When the cars themselves stop winning on numbers, brands sell everything around the number instead, and the ones that cannot lean on a badge or a vibe start cutting.

Bottom line: The winners this cycle are shoppers, especially in open markets, and the automakers who can make a cheap EV feel like enough. The losers are the squeezed middle: premium brands without a real moat, and legacy names learning that loyalty evaporates the moment a well built rival costs thousands less. Price is the whole story now. Everything else is marketing.
