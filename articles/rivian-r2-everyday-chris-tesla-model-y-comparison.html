Two Tesla Model Y Owners Drive the Rivian R2 and Keep Their Reservation

Everyday Chris  ·  Jun 10, 2026

Everyday Chris put down a Rivian R2 reservation two years ago. He has owned two Tesla Model Ys for the better part of a decade. The question he brought to Utah's launch event was concrete: is the R2 actually better, or does it just look good on paper? His conclusion was that the reservation stays. But the reasoning matters more than the headline, and it is more measured than the video title suggests. The R2 is not better than the Model Y across every axis. It is better in ways that are specific to a different kind of buyer, someone who values the capability to go off-road, wants more interior height and volume, and is willing to accept that Tesla's autonomy suite remains ahead for now.

The Model Y Performance starts at $57,990 and shares that price point with the R2 launch Performance trim before destination charges. Both are all-wheel drive. Both are rated around 330 miles of range in their respective EPA estimates. The Model Y Performance has tighter, more planted handling that one co-driver called "Macan EV levels." The R2 is softer, sits higher, and has significantly more headroom and cargo volume, with Rivian claiming 90.1 cubic feet of total storage versus the Model Y's 74.8. For buyers who spend most of their time on highways but want to take a mountain trail once a month, those are the numbers that actually matter.

The off-road section was a legitimate test. Chris admits he has limited trail experience, and the footage shows the R2 navigating loose rock and steep gradients with him doing very little beyond selecting all-terrain mode and staying on the throttle. Rally mode produced predictable rear-bias oversteer that both reviewers found usable and fun. The Rivian navigation system, including its Rivian-scored charger confidence ratings that only route customers to A and B grade stations, was called out as comparable to Tesla's integration in terms of route planning quality. The main remaining gap is autonomy: point-to-point navigation is promised by year-end 2026, but it is not in the car yet, and Chris was direct that if FSD is your primary reason to switch, you should wait or look elsewhere.

Bottom line: The most useful first-drive review for someone sitting at the R2-versus-Model-Y decision point, because that is exactly the situation the reviewer is in. The off-road footage is unscripted and the honesty about Tesla's autonomy lead is not buried. The R2 is the better adventure vehicle and the worse autonomous commuter. Those are not contradictory. They are just different use cases, and this video maps the boundary between them clearly.
