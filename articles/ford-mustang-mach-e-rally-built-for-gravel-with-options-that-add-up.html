The Ford Mustang Mach-E Rally Is Built for Gravel, With Options That Add Up

TFLEV  ·  Jul 14, 2026

Ford's most rugged electric Mustang is also its most playful. In a walkaround and drive from TFLEV, the 2026 Mustang Mach-E Rally is presented as a lifted, gravel-ready twist on the Mach-E GT, carrying 480 horsepower and 700 lb-ft of torque, with a zero to sixty time the hosts put at about 3.4 seconds. It runs a 91 kWh battery and a rated range of roughly 265 miles. The Rally package, which adds the raised suspension, the tuning, and the styling, costs about $4,300 on top of a car that starts near $61,000 and rang up around $63,000 as tested. What sets it apart is the mix: real speed, a taller stance, snow-rated tires, and a wing the hosts compare to a Focus RS, all in one wagonish crossover.

The Rally's headline feature is not power, it is the way Ford has kitted it for bad weather and loose surfaces. The hosts point out it ships on Michelin CrossClimate 2 tires, a three-peak snow-rated all-season, which they call an unusual factory choice and a genuine advantage somewhere like Colorado. That is the kind of practical detail that separates it from a standard performance crossover. Worth adding from outside the video: the wider United States charging market is moving to the North American Charging Standard, the Tesla connector, and the Mach-E still uses CCS and J1772, with a J3400 adapter sold separately. For a 2026 EV, that is a fair thing to weigh against rivals shipping native NACS. TFLEV also flags a pattern of paid extras that feel like they should be standard, including a $500 charge for the front trunk and a $145 inflation kit in place of a spare, which colors how the price reads. The hosts add that a J3400 adapter is a separate purchase, and they poke fun at the awkward Mach-E door handles that new passengers often need explained.

On the road, the hosts describe a car that is quick and composed rather than sharp. The 700 lb-ft of torque, according to the video, shoves hard from a stop, with a bit of front-end chirp when you launch it, and the MagneRide adaptive dampers let it ride soft for daily use while still tightening up for spirited driving. A Rally-specific mode the hosts call Rally Sport loosens the stability control to allow some sliding on loose surfaces, which they flag as the fun party trick, though they note this particular drive stayed on pavement. They like the wing-back front seats and the minimalist interior with its big physical volume knob, while calling the flat rear seats a little church-pew firm for long trips. On charging, the video lists a peak around 150 kW, which the hosts frame as merely okay next to rivals like the Hyundai Ioniq 5 XRT. Asked to choose between this and a Rivian R2 order, one host leans toward the R2. The walkaround also covers the Desert Sand paint with contrasting hood stripes, fog lights set into the faux grille, and roughly six inches more approach angle than a standard Mach-E, thanks to the taller ride height. Inside there are three drive modes the hosts name whisper, engage, and unbridled, and the Rally carries the MagneRide setup adapted from the GT.

Bottom line: The Mach-E Rally is the most charming Mustang Mach-E to look at and one of the more genuinely fun to drive, and if you live somewhere with real winters, those factory CrossClimate tires are not a gimmick. The catch is the nickel-and-diming. Charging $500 for a frunk and $145 for an inflation kit on a $63,000 car is the kind of thing that turns a good first impression sour, and the CCS-only setup dates it against newer NACS rivals. Buy it for the look and the all-weather hardware, and go in knowing the options sheet is where Ford gets you.
