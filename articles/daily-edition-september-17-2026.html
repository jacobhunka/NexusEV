Same Forecourt, Different Fuel

Norway's fuel stations are quietly becoming charging stops, and today's GTI, hydrogen X5, Mach-E and India stories show the same shift: familiar formats, new contents.

nexusEVnews  ·  Sep 17, 2026

On the E6 north of Oslo, Out of Spec Roaming noticed the same detail more than once: a bare patch of forecourt where a fuel pump used to stand. Late in the drive the host said he had yet to find a station that had not removed one. The building, the canopy and the highway exit all stayed put. What drivers pull in for is what changed. That is the pattern running through today's five stories. The formats of the petrol age are proving durable. What goes inside them is not.

Carmakers are leaning on that durability on purpose. Volkswagen is moving the GTI badge, a name built on front-drive petrol hatchbacks, onto the rear-drive ID.3, and Autogefühl's host thinks the break with tradition is a gain, because rear drive suits a sporty car better. BMW takes the idea further with the iX5 Hydrogen. In its own video, the company says the goal was an X5 that gives nothing away from the outside, with its hydrogen tanks slotted into the same space as BMW's Gen6 battery and every X5 drivetrain built on one production line. The shape customers already trust becomes the delivery vehicle for a powertrain most of them have never used.

Some inherited formats fit less comfortably. In a petrol car, a gauge reading empty meant something simple. In Out of Spec Testing's Mustang Mach-E Rally, zero on the display was followed by another 25.2 miles of driving. The host's explanation is that Ford's estimate of remaining charge can drift over long stretches without a rest, so a cushion below zero protects drivers from a reading that may be off. The dial looks familiar. What sits behind it is an estimate, and the car hedges around it.

The largest version of this is a whole economy. Industrialisation has usually run on fossil fuels, as Just Have a Think points out, and the channel weighs analyst Kingsmill Bond's claim, written for Yale Environment 360, that India is following the same path on sunshine instead. Its answer is that the claim holds for new supply and overstates the change for the grid as a whole, where coal is still growing and transmission lines lag behind the solar farms waiting to connect. From a distance, the development path looks the same as China's. The fuel underneath it is starting to differ.

What to watch: whether Norway's operators replace the older, slower chargers the host says are due for an upgrade; whether Volkswagen puts a power figure and a price on the ID.3 GTI, since Autogefühl's car was not yet running final software; whether BMW says where the iX5 Hydrogen will be sold, because a quick refill still needs a station on the route; whether the Mach-E's cushion holds up in the kind of higher-mileage retest the host says would be revealing; and whether India's power lines start keeping pace with its panels. Keeping the old shape is the easy part. Filling it is where these stories will be decided.

Review

Volkswagen Puts the GTI Badge on a Rear-Drive EV

Autogefühl

Autogefühl's Thomas toured the Volkswagen ID.3 GTI, which replaces the GTX as the sportiest version of the facelifted hatchback and which the video bills as the first rear-wheel-drive GTI. That layout comes from the MEB platform underneath, which is rear-drive by design. According to the video, the car runs 0 to 100 km/h in 5.6 seconds and tops out at 200 km/h, higher than the regular ID.3. There is one battery, 79 kWh net, with 11 kW AC charging and a 183 kW DC peak, though the host says a 10 to 80 percent charge still takes 29 minutes. He quotes a WLTP range above 600 km and expects a little over 400 km on the motorway. Inside, the facelift brings back four proper window switches and physical climate controls, and the GTI adds Clark-pattern seats, a red-trimmed wheel and a button that jumps straight to a GTI drive mode. Thomas says this car was not on final software, that new ESC settings are still in development, and that the electric GTIs will get synthetic engine sound without the simulated gear changes he describes in Hyundai's Ioniq N models. The video gives no power output or price, and that is the gap that matters: until both arrive, buyers cannot judge it against rival electric hot hatches on value.

Deep Dive

BMW Hides Seven Hydrogen Tanks Where the X5's Battery Goes

BMW Group

This is BMW's own explainer on the iX5 Hydrogen, so the figures in it are the carmaker's claims rather than independent test results. BMW says it developed a flat storage system that holds seven carbon-fibre-reinforced tanks, pressurised to 700 bar, inside a single metal frame under the floor. According to the video, the system stores more than 7 kg of hydrogen, fits the same space as BMW's Gen6 high-voltage battery, and refuels in under five minutes. A third-generation fuel cell turns the hydrogen into electricity, while a high-voltage battery adds power when needed and stores energy recovered under braking. BMW quotes a range of up to 750 km and says this is its first fuel cell car with its electric xDrive all-wheel-drive system. The bigger claim is about manufacturing: BMW says the hydrogen model shares its architecture with the combustion, plug-in hybrid and battery electric X5, so all of them can come down the same line. The video does not give a price, a launch date or the markets where the car will be sold. That last gap matters most, because a quick refill only helps drivers who have a hydrogen station nearby, and in most regions public stations are still scarce.

Charging

A Norway Road Trip Tests Whether Every Highway Stop Has Chargers

Out of Spec Roaming

Out of Spec Roaming drove north from Oslo on the E6 in a Mercedes GLC 400 4MATIC with EQ Technology and stopped at every highway stop with a fuel station, skipping bare rest areas. The host says Norway has had two years of EV sales above 90 percent, and he wanted to see whether the highway corridor keeps up. Almost every stop had fast chargers, often from several operators, with Tesla, Ionity, Circle K and Recharge among the names along the way, and Kempower and Alpitronic hardware common. The one exception, near Gardermoen, was a new Recharge site whose Alpitronic and Kempower units were not yet switched on, which he judged to be a fresh installation rather than a fault. Quality varies. He points to older Kempower units at one stop that only support 400-volt cars, and says Recharge has older hardware at some sites that, in his view, is due for an update. At the other end, he found truck chargers with a Scania electric truck plugged in, and an Alpitronic Hyper 1000 with a megawatt charging connector being installed beside the highway. He also criticised the GLC's lack of a simple keep-climate mode. For drivers in places where coverage is still patchy, the notable detail is how many stops had rival operators side by side, which the host says keeps pressure on price.

Solar

India Is Adding Sun Faster Than It Can Add Wires

Just Have a Think

Just Have a Think tests a line from Kingsmill Bond, energy strategist at the think tank Ember, and finds it partly right. The video's figures show solar capacity rising from roughly 100 GW at the end of 2024 to almost 165 GW by the end of July 2026. It cites Wood Mackenzie expecting more than 50 GW of new solar this year, and Ember putting clean sources at 27 percent of India's generation. The channel argues the current mix matters less than where new supply comes from. The International Energy Agency, as quoted in the video, expects demand to grow about six and a half percent a year to 2030, with solar covering roughly half of that increase. Developers are increasingly bidding solar, wind and batteries together, the video says, helped by cheap panels and falling storage costs. The catch is the grid. The video quotes researcher Charith Konda on solar farms taking 18 to 24 months to build while transmission lines take about five years, cites a Reuters report of more than 50 GW of projects left effectively stranded, and notes India dropped its annual tender target in January. Per the IEA figures shown, coal generation keeps growing even as its share falls toward 60 percent by 2030. Grid connections trailing cheap generation is a familiar problem in the US and Europe too, which makes India's scale the real test.

Battery Tech

The Mach-E Rally Kept Going 25 Miles Past Zero

Out of Spec Testing

Out of Spec Testing ran a 2026 Ford Mustang Mach-E Rally, with what Ford claims is a 91 kWh pack and about 1,500 miles on the clock, around a track until it stopped. Colleague William drove, starting at 5 percent. The video shows a power limit already in place, repeated low-range warnings, and navigation alerts that the nearest charger was first at the edge of range and then out of reach. After the display hit zero, the climate control switched itself off at 2.9 miles and could not be turned back on. A depleted-battery message told William to stop safely at 9.7 miles, yet he could still reach 55 mph on the straight past 20 miles. Power dropped sharply at 25.1 miles and the car shut down at 25.2, averaging 3.7 miles per kWh, which the channel's math puts at roughly 6 to 7 kWh below zero. The host cautions against reading much into one run. He suggests the big buffer may exist because Ford's charge estimate can drift over long drives without a rest, says the same car might not pass zero at all after more use, and advises owners to let the car sit parked before running it low. Most EVs also hold back a slice of the pack that drivers can never use, so a zero reading rarely means the cells are truly empty.

Bottom line: Familiar shapes are a smart way to sell new technology, but they only work if the contents deliver. The rear-drive ID.3 GTI is the most convincing case today, though without a price it is still a promise. BMW's hydrogen X5 is clever packaging still looking for a refuelling network. Mach-E owners should enjoy the hidden miles and never plan around them, since the host himself says they may shrink. And Norway shows the end state worth copying: the same old stop, a plug where the pump was, and no planning required.
