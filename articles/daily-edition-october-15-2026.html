What You See Is Not What Changed

An Atto 3 that looks the same and is not, an iX4 whose new roofline costs boot space, and a charging network that grew without getting more reliable.

nexusEVnews  ·  Oct 15, 2026

BYD kept the strings. When Electrify This sat in the Atto 3 Evo Premium, the host found the old car's unusual door handles still in place and its cabin strings simply recoloured black. That reads as a facelift. The spec sheet says otherwise: by the host's list, a new battery, rear-wheel drive, an 800-volt system, 220 kW DC charging against the base Dynamic's 110 kW, and a frunk the old front-drive car never had. The host ends the video unable to decide between calling it a refresh and calling it a new car. That indecision runs through all five of today's stories: the change you can see and the change that matters keep turning up in different places.

BMW's iX4 runs the other way. OnlyBimmers treats it as the X4's return, with a falling roofline and a longer wheelbase than the old car, yet most of what the host measures is what the shape costs next to the iX3: 30 litres less boot, a rear bench lowered to keep headroom under the glass roof, and a tailgate that swings higher, which matters in a low garage. Renault's Espace concept sits at the far end of the scale, all visible change and nothing disclosed underneath. Electrifying saw six identical seats, a swivelling middle row and steer-by-wire, and got no battery, range or charging figure at all. Renault says it designed the cabin first and the body around it. For a family car, that is the right order. It also lets the hardest questions wait.

The parts nobody photographs are increasingly the ones that decide ownership. BYD's Atto 8, by Out of Spec Roaming's account, is the first BYD-badged car to bring flash charging to Europe. The host also found its software a step behind other Chinese brands and says BYD's God's Eye driver assistance is not yet offered there. By that account, the battery has arrived ahead of the software. Paren's third-quarter report, discussed on State of Charge, shows the same split at network scale: more than 80,000 active US fast-charging ports, with ports and sessions each up about 26 percent on last summer, but a national reliability score that has barely moved in a year and sits just under 94 percent. Paren's co-founder says reliability is steady or rising in Florida, Texas and California, where the new hardware is going, and slipping where it is not. Building more chargers has grown the map. It has not yet raised the odds that the next one works.

Each story has a test coming that will show which change was the real one. For the Atto 3 Evo Premium it is a long drive, where 220 kW either appears at a highway charger or does not. For the iX4 it is the price gap to the iX3, which will decide whether 30 litres feels like a choice or a surcharge. Renault has to say what powers the Espace, and whether it will build one; the host says Renault currently reports no production plans, and does not believe it. BYD has to bring its European software up to its hardware. And Paren's fourth-quarter report, where its co-founder expects a late-year burst of openings, is worth reading for the reliability line before the port count.

Renault Designed Its Electric Espace Concept From the Seats Outward

Electrifying

Electrifying's host had roughly ten minutes on the stand with Renault's Espace concept, an electric revival of the people carrier the host recalls arriving in 1984 and leaving UK showrooms around 2012. Renault says the cabin came first and the body was drawn around it. That cabin holds six identical seats in three rows of two on a flat floor, behind doors that open away from each other with no pillar between them. In the host's walkthrough, the middle row swivels to face the back, the seats fold into a table, and rear passengers get 12.3-inch screens. The driver faces a curved display about a metre across and a steer-by-wire wheel with no mechanical link to the steering. Outside, the host gives the length as 4.7 metres, close to family SUVs such as BMW's iX3, and the width as just over 2 metres, on 23-inch wheels. There is no battery, range or charging information. If it reaches production, the host would set it against Kia's PV5, a useful yardstick, since carwow was already weighing the PV5's purchase and lease costs in September.

Charging

US Fast Charging Passes 80,000 Ports, but Paren Finds Reliability Stuck Near 94 Percent

State of Charge

State of Charge host Tom Moloughney talked through Paren's third-quarter 2026 report with a co-founder of the company, which tracks US public fast charging. A disclosure the video makes itself: Moloughney wrote a guest opinion page in this quarter's report. As discussed, the US passed 80,000 active public fast-charging ports for the first time, and ports and charging sessions each grew about 26 percent from last summer, so use per port held steady. Reliability is where progress stalled: just under 94 percent nationally, little changed in a year. By Paren's count, Tesla added 1,287 ports in the quarter and IONNA 378, a total that includes Circle K sites IONNA re-equipped after buying them; without those, Walmart's 344 would rank second. The host says Tesla's share of new ports this year is 49.3 percent, below half for the first time. The host also cites Tesla's non-member Supercharger rate as up 15 percent in a year, to about 57 cents a kWh against roughly 39 for Tesla owners, while the national average Paren tracks edged from 53.8 to 54.0 cents. That puts it above the 50 cents a kWh Electric Era charged at the Colorado Love's site Out of Spec visited this month.

Review

BYD's Atto 3 Evo Premium Hides an 800-Volt, Rear-Drive Rebuild Behind a Familiar Cabin

Electrify This

Electrify This drove the Australian Atto 3 Evo in its top Premium trim, which the host prices just under A$51,000 drive-away, about A$5,000 above the Dynamic. By the host's figures, the Dynamic is front-drive on a 400-volt system with 110 kW DC and 7 kW AC charging and 420 km of range. The Premium adds a new battery, rear-wheel drive and 800 volts, for 220 kW DC, 11 kW AC, 510 km and a frunk. The cabin keeps its strings and unusual door handles under a calmer colour scheme, and adds an opening glass roof with an electric shade. The host's complaints: regeneration that stops short of one-pedal driving, heavy steering that is not especially sharp, and a driver monitor that nags and has misjudged speed. The ride is firmer than Geely's EX5, and the host urges Geely to hurry its refreshed, rear-drive EX5 to Australia in response. For buyers, the step up is easy to price: A$5,000 buys 90 km more quoted range, double the DC peak and the frunk. The host suggests some shoppers will not cross A$50,000, which leaves the Dynamic to hold that end of the market.

News

BYD's Atto 8 Brings Flash Charging to Europe, but Not God's Eye Yet

Out of Spec Roaming

Out of Spec Roaming got a first look at the BYD Atto 8, sold in China as the Tang L, which the host calls the first flash-charging model in Europe to wear a BYD badge rather than a Denza one. The host says it can charge from 10 to 97 percent in nine minutes on a flash charger; no price was given. It is a seven-seater, and in the host's view the best BYD-badged interior so far, with soft-touch trim reaching the third row, massaging and cooled second-row seats, a fridge set to minus 6°C, a powered frunk and an electric tow hook. The third row is compact; at 176 cm the host sat with knees angled up, though the host rates it roomier than a seven-seat Model Y. Against that, the screens showed glare, the software felt behind other Chinese brands, and a navigation test returned no charging stops. God's Eye driver assistance is not yet offered in Europe, the host says. One caution for buyers: the nine-minute figure has been checked before. A German lab test this site covered on September 26 reproduced BYD's five-minute charge but not the nine-minute one, by that video's account.

Review

BMW's iX4 Pays for Its Sloping Roof With 30 Litres of Boot and a Lower Rear Bench

OnlyBimmers

OnlyBimmers walked around the iX4 M60 in matte Frozen Pure Red. The host says every iX4 comes with the M Sport package and all-wheel drive, and that the M60 adds a stronger rear motor, a 20 kW boost button and adaptive M suspension as standard. That suspension will also be offered on all-wheel-drive iX3s, and the host says BMW made the base setup more comfortable after feedback that the iX3 rode roughly. The iX3 review from savagegeese earlier this month made a similar complaint about the ride for the price. The shape has measurable costs. The host puts the drag coefficient at 0.23 on 21-inch wheels, better than the iX3, but the boot holds 490 litres, 30 fewer than the iX3, and the rear bench sits lower to keep headroom under the glass roof, standard here and optional on the iX3, which leaves long legs at a worse angle. The tailgate also opens higher, though its height can be capped in the settings for low garages. No price, battery size or range was given, and for families with a stroller the host suggests the iX3 may still be the smarter pick.

Bottom line: The Atto 3 Evo Premium is the easiest recommendation here, because its upgrades are the dull, invisible kind that matter on a road trip: charging, drive layout and range, for just under A$51,000. The iX4 asks buyers to pay for a roofline, and until BMW prices it, the iX3 is the sensible default. The Espace concept is a cabin worth building around a drivetrain nobody has described. The Atto 8 needs its software to catch up with its battery. And the next US charging milestone that matters is not 100,000 ports. It is reliability above 95 percent.
