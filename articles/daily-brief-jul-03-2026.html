The Showcase and the Business Case

nexusEVnews Daily Brief  ·  Jul 3, 2026

ABB E-mobility's Chris Thompson dropped a golfer's line at the ACT show this week: peak power is for the showcase, continuous power is for the business case. It is a tidy way to describe the tension running through all five of today's stories. The headline numbers, megawatts and 1,200 horsepower and three-digit range ratings, are the show. The engineering that decides what actually sells is quieter, and it is mostly about scale and cost.

Start with the two extremes. ABB is building charging hardware that can hit the megawatt level, but the whole pitch is to stop buying for peak and size a site to how it will really be used, because idle conversion capacity is what wrecks charging economics. At the other end sits the McLaren W1, which is pure show: 1,200 horsepower, 399 units, two million dollars, a poster car almost nobody reading this will buy. Both are impressive. Only one has a business case, and it is not the one with the active rear wing.

The middle three stories are where the business case lives. Tesla's Model Y L does not chase a giant battery; it stretches an efficient platform to add a third row and still posts competitive range from 83 kWh. Rivian's R2 range test shows the same discipline in reverse: the truck keeps a cheaper 400-volt architecture and leans on efficiency, and even on all-terrain tires up a mountain it cleared 260 miles. And Utah's plug-in solar is the smallest bet of all, an 800-dollar kit meant to shave a power bill, held back not by physics but by a safety standard that has not cleared committee.

What to watch over the next six months is which of these business cases converts. Charging economics, cell cost, and standards approvals are unglamorous, but they are where EVs get won or lost now that the core technology mostly works. The show cars will keep getting the clicks. The putting is where the money is.

Bottom line: The cars and chargers built around continuous, everyday economics, the Model Y L, the R2, distributed charging, cheap plug-in solar, are the ones quietly reshaping the market. The megawatt demo and the hypercar are the trailer. The feature is efficiency, price, and paperwork, and that is the part worth paying attention to.
