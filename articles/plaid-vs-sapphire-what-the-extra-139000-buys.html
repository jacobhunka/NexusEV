Plaid vs Sapphire: What the Extra $139,000 Buys

Ever  ·  Aug 7, 2026

Two American sedans, both making over a thousand horsepower, separated by roughly $139,000. Ever lines up a Tesla Model S Plaid against a Lucid Air Sapphire and works through what that money actually changes. The video puts the Plaid at 1,020 horsepower and the Sapphire at 1,234, both using a three motor layout with a single motor at the front and two at the rear. The 2026 Plaid started at $111,630 when new, according to the video, while the Sapphire launched at $250,500. Both are large, practical sedans with real trunks and real back seats. The interesting result is how little the price difference buys in a straight line, and how much it buys everywhere else.

Straight line acceleration has stopped being the separator at this level, so the comparison lands on other things. Ever measured two 0 to 60 mph runs in the Sapphire at 2.27 seconds each on a normal road surface, and notes that Tesla's own 1.99 second claim for the Plaid depends on a prepped drag surface, the one foot rollout convention, and the car's launch stance. Real world, the video estimates 2.2 to 2.3 seconds for the Plaid. That is close enough that a buyer is really choosing between two ecosystems. The video points out the Sapphire offers wireless Apple CarPlay and Android Auto and physical climate toggles, which matters if you would rather not live entirely inside one manufacturer's software. There is also a used market angle worth knowing: Ever says 2026 Plaids are now hovering around $150,000 after the model was discontinued, while 2021 and 2022 cars can be found in the $50,000 range.

On hardware, the video reports the Plaid uses a 100 kWh pack with 95 kWh usable, rated at 396 miles on 19 inch wheels or 348 miles on 21s, with a 250 kW peak charging rate and roughly 30 minutes from 10 to 80 percent. The Sapphire runs 119 kWh total and 118 usable, an EPA rating of 427 miles, a 300 kW peak and 29 minutes for the same charge window. Ever notes an independent 70 mph range test that returned closer to 340 miles and says Lucid's own estimates tend to run optimistic. The Sapphire gets carbon ceramic brakes as standard while the Plaid uses cast iron, with the video adding that Tesla's $20,000 track package is sold out. Running gear differs too: air suspension on the Tesla, steel springs with adaptive dampers on the Lucid. On the road, the host calls the Sapphire the most impressive car he has driven, singling out steering feel and body control, while defending the Plaid as far more fun than its reputation suggests. Regeneration differs as well, with the video citing up to 250 kW in the Sapphire against 200 kW in the Plaid. Inside, the Tesla gets a 17 inch tilting center screen, a 22 speaker audio system and the yoke wheel, which the host says he has adapted to without preferring. The Lucid answers with 18 way massaging front seats, soft close doors and noticeably more rear legroom despite being the shorter car. Cargo splits the difference: 25 cubic feet behind the Plaid's liftgate plus 3.1 in the frunk, against 22 cubic feet in the Sapphire's trunk and a 10 cubic foot front compartment. Warranty coverage favors Tesla at eight years or 150,000 miles on the powertrain versus eight years or 100,000 miles.

Bottom line: The Sapphire is the better driving car and it should be, at more than double the money. What the video really exposes is how much of that experience the Plaid delivers for a fraction of the price, especially on the used market. If you are buying with your head, a three or four year old Plaid at a third of a Sapphire's launch price is close to unbeatable value for the performance. If you are buying because you want the best steering and ride quality any American EV has managed so far, the Lucid earns it. Just do not pretend the extra $139,000 is about speed.
