Two Ways to Sell a $30,000 EV

nexusEVnews Daily Brief  ·  Jul 16, 2026

Two electric cars landed at roughly the same price today and almost nothing else about them matches. The Chevrolet Bolt reached $29,000 on purpose, after years of engineering a car down to a number. The Polestar 4 reached $31,000 because somebody has to move the metal before a deadline. One of those is a business model. The other is a fire sale wearing the same price tag, and telling them apart is most of what an EV buyer needs to know right now.

Car and Driver drove 25 electric cars over the same roads and gave its EV of the Year award to the cheapest one, on the strength of an iron phosphate pack, a 262 mile EPA rating and a dashboard with actual buttons. Nothing about that car is exotic. That is the achievement. Meanwhile a 544 horsepower luxury crossover has been marked down $25,000 because, the video says, its maker is leaving the market by 2027. The Bolt's price is a floor somebody built. The Polestar's price is a hole somebody fell into.

The pattern underneath both is that a car's value is decided long after the sale, and today's other stories are all about that afterlife. NHK reports a Japanese dealer selling 2.6 times more used EVs for one reason: he can finally show a buyer what the battery has left. Out of Spec found a performance badge that lost its track mode when the car was updated, which is the same lesson from the other direction. What you paid for and what you own can drift apart, and increasingly the drift is set by data and code rather than by the metal.

In Britain, buyers appear to have stopped waiting for any of this to be settled. New battery EV sales ran close to ten to one against pure petrol cars in the year to May, according to the SMMT figures cited in that video. Watch the next six months for whether the $29,000 EV gets company. If it does, the discount stories stop being news. If it does not, every affordable EV in America will be somebody's clearance event, and that is not a market, it is a going-out-of-business sign with good financing attached.

Bottom line: Buy the Polestar 4 if you keep cars for a decade and can live with Volvo service. But the Bolt is the more important car, because it is the only one of the two that can be built again next year at the same price. A bargain that depends on somebody's retreat is not a bargain the industry can repeat, and $29,000 done deliberately beats $31,000 done in a panic.
