Tesla's Model Y L Reaches North America With Six Seats and One Awkward Row

Out of Spec Reviews  ·  Aug 1, 2026

The stretched Model Y has landed in North America, and Out of Spec Reviews has been through it in detail at Tesla's launch event in Los Angeles. The Model Y L runs seven inches longer than the standard car on a six inch longer wheelbase, with a bigger battery built from four long Panasonic modules instead of two, which raises pack voltage as well as capacity. Tesla quotes more than 325 miles, though the video notes the car has not been EPA rated yet. Also quoted: 4.4 seconds to 60 mph, 496 peak horsepower, 250 kW peak charging, 3,500 pounds of towing, a 4,612 pound curb weight and 1,213 pounds of payload. Deliveries are expected to begin in September, with the launch edition sitting just above $60,000.

The odd part of the product is the seating. The Model Y L is a six seat car only, while the shorter standard Model Y is offered with seven. A vehicle positioned explicitly as the family option therefore carries one fewer seat than the cheaper car it sits above. Two details are worth pulling out for buyers. The video notes that curb weight and payload put the car under 6,000 pounds gross, which rules out the Section 179 deduction some business buyers rely on with heavier SUVs, and that is a real financial consideration rather than a piece of trivia. The second is that the 325 mile figure is a manufacturer estimate. Anyone ordering now commits before an independent rating exists, which is worth knowing even if the number proves accurate. The hosts are also direct that this is not the large three row electric SUV Tesla buyers keep asking for, pointing at the Rivian R1S and Lucid Gravity as the cars that actually occupy that space.

Most of the engineering effort is invisible. The video covers a second generation thermal management unit that shares its compressor with the previous system but adds roughly 15 percent more cooling capacity, along with a larger desiccant filter rated for around eight years. Tesla's own glass division supplies a solar reflective coating on the roof and hatch, and the hosts describe a side by side demonstration against an older Model Y under a heat lamp as the most convincing thing they saw all day. Suspension uses the same single valve adaptive dampers as the Model Y Performance with different springs and tuning, plus a rear comfort mode that shifts the car's pitch centre toward the second row, a feature the video says was developed primarily for chauffeur-driven buyers in China. Tyres are staggered at 255 front and 275 rear. The criticism is consistent and specific: the second row tilts but does not slide, so reaching the third row means going down the middle. The hosts also note the US car loses the cooled wireless phone chargers fitted to the Chinese version. Elsewhere the detail is strong: a 19 speaker audio system the presenter rates among the best he has heard, B-pillar and C-pillar vents feeding the rear rows, reclining third row seats, and 65 watt USB-C in the second row with two 27 watt outlets in the third. Two children brought along for the test reported no complaints about space, airflow or heat.

Bottom line: If you were already buying a Model Y and you have kids, this is the one to buy, and the launch edition sitting just above $60,000 is not a stretch over a well specced standard car. What it is not is the large three row electric SUV a lot of people keep asking Tesla for. This is a stretched compact crossover doing a competent impression of one. Families with three or more children, or anyone who regularly carries adults in the back row, should still be shopping elsewhere.
