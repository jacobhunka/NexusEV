Could You Live Full Time in an Electric VW Camper Van?

The Endless Adventure  ·  Jul 18, 2026

The Endless Adventure spends three days trying to live full time in an electric Volkswagen ID Buzz that has been converted into a camper, complete with a small kitchen, a pop top, and dual workspaces. The couple picks it up in Seattle and heads into Washington's Olympic Peninsula, where the honest verdict arrives fast: the modern hardware is lovely, but the space is tiny and the range takes planning. They wake on day one to 30% battery after a charging cable seated wrong overnight, then spend much of a morning crawling back up at about 14 miles of range per hour on a slow connection. It is a fair, unglamorous look at what an electric adventure van actually asks of you, quirks and all.

The US ID Buzz is the long wheelbase, three row version of Volkswagen's Microbus revival, and its EPA range is rated at 234 miles for rear drive or 231 with all wheel drive, figures reviewers widely call modest for a vehicle this size. That ceiling is the whole story of this trip. A camper conversion adds weight and drag, and the couple notes that estimated range roughly halves on a sustained climb, which is exactly what happens on the way up to Hurricane Ridge in Olympic National Park. For anyone cross shopping an electric van against a diesel Sprinter build, that is the tradeoff: near silent, breakdown free driving and clean power at camp, set against a real need to map chargers at both ends of any remote leg. It is a lifestyle vehicle first and a long haul road trip tool second, and this video is useful precisely because it tests the second job the marketing tends to skip.

The video is candid about the daily friction. The hosts say the automatic doors sometimes will not open, and that with no engine noise they cannot always tell the van is on until they press the accelerator. On a Tesla Supercharger with an adapter they report going from a low state of charge to 80% in about 20 minutes, far quicker than the campsite trickle that would have taken until late at night. Inside, they praise the fridge and the two separate workspaces but keep hitting the same wall, storage. The fresh and waste tanks are only about three gallons each, so the sink fills fast, the bed is a nightly wrestling match in a cramped space, and clutter piles up in seconds. Their most reassuring finding is about power at camp: the onboard house battery dropped only about 30% overnight even off grid, so a night or two away from a plug is comfortable. The limiting factor is living space, not electricity. Their itinerary underlines the point: a run to the town of Forks, a rocky Pacific beach, a climb to Hurricane Ridge, and a Thai dinner interrupted by breaching whales, all while babysitting the state of charge. By the end they suggest the pop top bed upstairs is roomier than the lower berth, and that anyone spending real time in the van would want bigger fresh and waste tanks than the three gallon units fitted here.

Bottom line: This is a great weekend machine and a questionable full time home. The Endless Adventure lands on roughly three days, maybe a week, before the small space wins, and that feels right. If your trips are short hops between towns with charging at each end, the ID Buzz camper delivers the Microbus fantasy with none of the old mechanical anxiety. If you want to disappear into the backcountry for a week, the 234 mile rating and three gallon tanks will run the show, not you. Buy it for the vibe and the short adventures, not the great escape.
