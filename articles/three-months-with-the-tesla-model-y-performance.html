Three Months In, Is the Model Y Performance Worth the Extra Money?

Everyday Chris  ·  Jul 6, 2026

Everyday Chris has lived with a Tesla Model Y Performance for a bit over three months, and the question driving his review is simple: does the roughly 8,000 dollar step up from a standard Model Y actually make sense? He grades it on driving, features and tech, and value, then runs the tests he is known for, including a full-cup water challenge and a blindfolded ride comparison against the cheaper car. The Performance lists at 57,990 dollars, against 45,990 for the rear-wheel-drive Model Y and 49,990 for all-wheel drive. It rides about an inch lower, wears staggered 21-inch wheels, adds a bolted-on carbon spoiler, and brings a sport handling mode the standard car does not have. Range drops to a claimed 306 miles versus 327 on the premium.

The interesting tension is that the Performance costs more but asks for compromises a daily driver may not want. Chris reports the staggered tire setup cannot be rotated normally, and shows visible wear at only about 1,300 miles, against a standard Model Y that still had healthy tread near 14,000 miles. He also flags an insurance jump, saying State Farm quoted 3,600 dollars a year for the Performance versus 2,400 for the premium. For context the video does not dwell on: cheaper consumables and insurance are exactly where a hot-rod EV quietly costs you after the sticker, and it is worth stacking the Performance against a Rivian R2 Performance, which he notes carries the same 57,990 price, before deciding the badge is worth it.

On the road, the split is clear. In sport mode Chris says handling tightens up noticeably and body roll shrinks, while standard mode keeps things soft enough for passengers, and the launch is quick enough that his water-cup test ends in a soaked cabin. He measured 69.8 decibels at 60 miles per hour in the Performance against about 66 in the premium, a small penalty from the bigger wheels. A charging test from 27 to 80 percent at a 250 kilowatt Supercharger showed the Performance taking a few minutes longer than the premium, and the premium proving slightly more efficient overall. He also notes early build issues from the Austin car, including a window squeak and a trunk gap, both fixed under warranty.

Beyond the driving, Chris spends time on the parts that are shared with the cheaper car and the parts that are not. He demonstrates the Performance seats, which add a thigh bolster he says the standard Model Y does not get but the new Model Y L does, along with heating and cooling and firmer side support. He runs the 600-watt vehicle-to-load outlet, shows the sealed and drainable frunk, and points out carbon-fiber trim as one of the few interior giveaways. On value, the video stacks the 57,990-dollar Performance against rivals: he cites a Rivian R2 Performance at the same price, a Ford Mustang Mach-E starting around 50,000 dollars, and a Porsche Macan EV starting near 86,000. His read is that the Tesla undercuts the premium badges while still offering the launch-mode party trick, even if the steering wheel and some driver-assist features feel unchanged from the standard car.

Bottom line: The title is a bit of a troll, because Chris clearly loves driving the thing. His honest read is the one to trust: if you want the emotion, the launch, and the sport handling, the Performance delivers and he would buy it again. For most people using a Model Y as a family car and a commuter, the premium is already good enough, and the Performance adds faster tire wear and a real insurance premium on top of the 8,000 dollars. Pick it with your heart, not your spreadsheet.
