Cheap by Design, Cheap by Default

nexusEVnews Daily Brief  ·  Jul 20, 2026

There are two ways to make an electric car cheap. You can engineer the cost out before it reaches the showroom, or you can let the market bleed the price down after the fact. Today's stories split cleanly along that line, and the split says a lot about where each region is headed.

Europe is getting the engineered kind. The Geely EX2 is a Chinese best-seller reworked for European roads and priced from about 21,000 pounds, cheap on purpose, and it charmed a reviewer who expected to dislike it. Volkswagen's answer, the ID Cross, starts near 28,000 euros and tries to win on space and cabin quality rather than a race to the bottom. Both are built to hit a price, not marked down to reach one.

America is getting the other kind. The Audi RS e-tron GT was a $147,000 car that now trades roughly $100,000 lower, cheap because it depreciated hard, not because anyone designed it to be attainable. GM's electric pickups are cheap because they are not selling, with up to $21,400 off a Silverado EV that dealers cannot move. That is affordability by discount, and it only lasts as long as the oversupply does.

Which brings in the day's featured conversation. Kim Java's panel argues the US only gets the discount-bin version of cheap because it has walled out the engineered kind. Block the low-cost imports, and the domestic market never feels the pressure that forces prices, and products, to improve. The affordable EVs Americans can buy are last generation's flagships and this year's slow sellers, not next year's purpose-built bargains.

What to watch over the next six months is whether that gap widens. As engineered-cheap Chinese models spread across Europe and reset expectations there, American buyers will keep reading about small city EVs they cannot purchase. The pressure will build on the tariff wall, not because policy shifts on its own, but because consumers start to notice what they are missing.

Bottom line: Cheap by design beats cheap by default every time, because one is a strategy and the other is a symptom. Europe is choosing its price. America is discovering it. Until that changes, the best EV deals in the US will keep coming from the clearance rack, not the drawing board.
